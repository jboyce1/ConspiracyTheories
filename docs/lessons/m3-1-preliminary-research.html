    Preliminary Research

     

    This is where you will collect your initial research for your conspiracy theory. The step is not a deep dive into any particular source or specific theory, but a broad sweep of all the content that you can find. You will start a document that is designed to collect and organize your research, and this should direct your "deep dive" research later on.

     

     

    Steps for finding sources:

    1. Do a basic search for your topic, read an overview (tertiary source) and write down any vocabulary that may help in your subsequent searches.
    2. In a new browser, open a word document and Start a SourceDoc by creating starting to capture good search terms relevant to your search
    3. In a new browser, use Google or another search engine to search for institutional information ( “sEaRcH tErM site:.edu” or “sEaRcH tErM site:.gov”). Open as many as seem promising in new tabs.
    4. In a new browser, use Google or another search engine to search for non-institutional specific information ( “sEaRcH tErMs"). Open as many sites as seem promising in new tabs.
    5. Log into your local library site and search the catalog, or the associated
    6. Now that you have an obnoxious amount of tabs open, it is time to start winnowing the field and deleting some options. Look at each site and determine whether it contains information relevant to your research. If it does not close out the tab
    7. For the remainder of the sites, close any tab that does not pass the credibility/validity test.
    8. Create a Source Document by copying and pasting all relevant information into a single document.
    9. Create Chicago style citations for each source you plan on using
      1. End notes are the best for most historical research and papers
      2. Footnotes are acceptable if you are using Google Docs

    SourceDoc Example

    Credibility/Validity Score

    • Legitimacy of the Institution (Institution Score):
      • 1: Highly reputable institution
      • 0.5: Mostly reputable, but with minor concerns
      • 0: Neutral or unknown institution
      • -0.5: Somewhat unreliable, often biased or lacking credibility

      • -1: Known for misinformation or clear bias

         

    • Author’s Expertise (Expertise Score):

      • 1: Author is an expert in the field
      • 0.5: Author shows some expertise but lacks full credibility
      • 0: Author has moderate expertise or is unclear
      • -0.5: Author lacks significant expertise, with some unreliable information
      • -1: Author has no relevant expertise or has a history of presenting false information

       

    • Objectivity of the Source (Objectivity Score):
      • 1: Completely objective and balanced
      • 0.5: Mostly objective, with minor bias or omissions
      • 0: Neutral but lacks depth or multiple viewpoints
      • -0.5: Somewhat biased, with an agenda or slant

      • -1: Clearly biased, with a strong agenda

         

    • Intended Audience (Audience Score):
      • 1: Aimed at an educated, critical audience
      • 0.5: Aimed at a knowledgeable audience, but with some simplifications
      • 0: Aimed at a general audience
      • -0.5: Aimed at an audience prone to bias or sensationalism
      • -1: Aimed at a sensationalist or conspiracy-driven audience

    For each source, add the scores and divide by 4 to give an overall average. This score should go between the title and the citation in your SourceDoc
