    What is a Conspiracy Theory

    Definitions

    conspiracy

    conspiracy noun con·​spir·​a·​cy 
    1: the act of conspiring together
    2: an agreement among conspirators

    conspire

    1: to join in a secret agreement to do an unlawful or wrongful act or an act which becomes unlawful as a result of the secret agreement

    2: to act in harmony toward a common end

    Circumstances conspired to defeat his efforts.

    theory

     A scheme or system of ideas or statements held as an explanation or account of a group of facts or phenomena. (An explanation of why something behaves the way it does.)

    law

    A set of observed regularities expressed in a concise verbal or mathematical statement. (A reliable and repeatable observation of somethings behavior)

    Goal:

    In groups of 3-4, set a timer for 8 minutes, and come up with as many unique conspiracy theories as you can think of. Stretch your thinking beyond traditional conspiracy theories and consider anything that would fit the definitions.

    First Broadcast

    In this section you will complete research independently based on a selected/assigned conspiracy theory from the brainstorming sessions. Once the “Conspiracy Theory Research Worksheet” is completed, put responses into the templates, making adjustments so that your script flows well. Then you record your script using Audacity.  Once your recording is complete and uploaded, it will be added to the end of my introduction.

    Conspiracy Theory Research Worksheet

    1. Conspiracy Theory Overview:

    • Name of the Conspiracy Theory:
    • Brief Description of the Theory 

    2. Key People or Groups Involved: Who are the main people or groups associated with this theory?

    3. Main Claim or Belief: What is the central claim or belief of this conspiracy theory?

    4. Origin and Popularity:

    • When did this theory originate or become popular?
    • Where is this theory most commonly associated with or said to have taken place?

    5. Reasons and Motivations: Why do supporters believe in this theory? What motivates them?

    6. Explanation of the Theory: How does the theory suggest that the conspiracy works or took place?

    7. Evidence or Arguments: What evidence or arguments do supporters use to back up this theory?

    8. Counterarguments or Skepticism: What do critics or skeptics say to debunk this theory?

    9. Interesting Facts: What is one interesting or surprising fact you discovered during your research?

    10. Summary: How would you briefly summarize this conspiracy theory?

    11. Personal Reflection (Optional): What are your thoughts on this conspiracy theory? Do you find it convincing or not? Why?

    Post Scripting Discussion Questions

    From Lizard People Overlords to the NSA's PRISM program, what should be considered a conspiracy?

    From the “popular kids” to friends planning to make someone look bad, are there conspiracies in your life? 

    When does skepticism become unhealthy? How can we differentiate between healthy questioning and falling into conspiracy thinking?

    How does the spread of conspiracy theories impact trust in government, science, and media? What are the consequences of widespread belief in a false conspiracy?

    Post Recording Discussion Questions

    Why do some conspiracy theories gain more popularity than others? What role do social media and the internet play in amplifying certain conspiracies?

    What role does fear play in the creation and spread of conspiracy theories? How might fear of the unknown drive people to seek out these explanations?

    Can conspiracy theories ever be beneficial to society? For example, can they serve as a form of social criticism or bring attention to real issues that are otherwise ignored?

    Why are some people more likely to believe in multiple conspiracy theories, even if those theories contradict each other? What does this say about human psychology?

    In what ways do conspiracy theories challenge or reinforce social norms and values? Can believing in a conspiracy theory ever be seen as an act of rebellion or conformity?
