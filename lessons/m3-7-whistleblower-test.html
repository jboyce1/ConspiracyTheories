    M3.7 · Conspiracy Theories

    Whistleblower Test

    The Whistle Blower test is a consideration of the odds that your hypothesis would be exposed by someone who is supposed to have kept it secret. This may seem fundamentally flawed because we only have the one side of the data to work with, meaning we cannot account for conspiracies that have never been found out or that had been exposed in ways other than whistle blowers. However, we can look at the pattern of whistle blowing and apply that pattern to your hypothesis with a reasonable estimation that the odds of whistleblowing is consistent in consistent circumstances. 

    Historical Case Studies

    Case 1: Watergate Scandal (1972)

    • Conspiracy Type: Government
    • Number of People Involved: At least 69 individuals indicted, including the President and top aides.
    • Duration: 2 years (1972-1974)
    • How Exposed: Whistleblower "Deep Throat" (FBI Associate Director Mark Felt).

    Case 2: My Lai Massacre Cover-Up (1968)

    • Conspiracy Type: Military
    • Number of People Involved: Approximately 30 soldiers and officers were aware or involved.
    • Duration: 1 year (1968-1969)
    • How Exposed: Whistleblower Ronald Ridenhour wrote letters to Congress and the Pentagon.

    Case 3: Pentagon Papers (1971)

    • Conspiracy Type: Government
    • Number of People Involved: Over 1,000 analysts, policymakers, and military personnel contributed to classified studies.
    • Duration: 3 years of secrecy following the Vietnam War study.
    • How Exposed: Whistleblower Daniel Ellsberg leaked classified documents.

    Case 4: NSA Surveillance (2013)

    • Conspiracy Type: Government Intelligence
    • Number of People Involved: Thousands of employees and contractors with security clearance.
    • Duration: 6 years (2007-2013)
    • How Exposed: Whistleblower Edward Snowden released classified information.

    Hypothetical Analysis – Hitler's Survival Theory

    Scenario: Suppose Adolf Hitler survived World War II and fled to South America, living in secrecy until his death decades later.

    Analysis:

    1. Number of People Required:
      • Minimum Estimate: 20-30 (bodyguards, physicians, and confidants).
      • Maximum Estimate: 500+ (government officials, military, and intelligence personnel).
    2. Duration of Secrecy: At least 50 years.
    3. Whistleblower Probability: Using Dr. Robert Grimes' model, the probability of exposure increases exponentially with the number of people involved and time.
      • Small Group Estimate (30 people): 97% chance of exposure within 10 years.
      • Large Group Estimate (500 people): Near 100% chance of exposure within 10 years.
    4. Conclusion: Maintaining secrecy for over 50 years would be statistically improbable based on historical patterns of whistleblowing.
