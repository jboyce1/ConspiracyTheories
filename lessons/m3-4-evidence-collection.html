    Evidence Collection

    Evidence Collection Phase for the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)

    Purpose of Evidence Collection in ACH: The evidence collection phase in the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses is a crucial step designed to rigorously test and support your hypotheses. By meticulously gathering and assessing evidence, you can determine the strength and validity of your competing theories, ultimately guiding you towards the most plausible conclusion.

    ACH workflow.pptx

    Evidence Discussion

    • What is Evidence?
      • What do you think qualifies as evidence in an investigation? Can you give examples of good and bad evidence?
    • Sources of Evidence:
      • Why is it important to have multiple sources for your evidence? How does having more than one source help validate the information?
    • Evaluating Sources:
      • What criteria would you use to decide whether a source of evidence is reliable or not? Consider factors like authority, bias, and timeliness.
    • Impact of Biased Evidence:
      • What happens if the evidence collected is biased? How can biased evidence affect the outcome of an analysis?
    • Importance of Contradicting Evidence:
      • Why is it important to find evidence that contradicts your hypothesis? How does this help in making a balanced argument?
    • Role of Evidence in Hypotheses:
      • How does evidence support or weaken a hypothesis? Can you think of a situation where evidence changed your mind about something?
    • Scoring Evidence:
      • If you were to score evidence from -0.99 to 0.99 based on its reliability, what factors would influence your scoring?
    • Key Assumptions:
      • Why is it necessary to check the key assumptions behind your hypothesis? How can overlooked assumptions impact your conclusions?
    • Group Work in Evidence Collection:
      • How can working in a group improve the process of collecting and analyzing evidence? What challenges might you face while working with others on this task?
    • Reflection on the Process:
      • After collecting and analyzing evidence, what would you do differently in the next investigation? What lessons have you learned about the importance of thorough and critical examination of evidence?

    Evidence Collection Process

     Steps for Effective Evidence Collection:

    1. Identify Evidence Types:
      • Supporting Evidence: Gather at least two strong pieces of evidence that substantiate each hypothesis.
      • Contradicting Evidence: Identify at least one robust piece of evidence that challenges each hypothesis.
    2. Detailed Documentation:
      • Describe each piece of evidence in detail, ensuring that you capture all relevant aspects that support or contradict your hypotheses.
    3. Evidence Vetting Criteria:
      • Multiple Independent Sources: Confirm whether the evidence is supported by multiple independent sources, reducing the reliance on single-source information.
      • Reproduced Data: Assess if the data supporting the evidence has been independently reproduced and scrutinized, which enhances its reliability.
      • Reliable Source: Evaluate the credibility of the source providing the evidence. Consider if the source has authority on the topic or backing from a professional organization.
      • Dispassionate Source: Determine if the source has potential biases or a stake in the outcome. A dispassionate source is more likely to provide unbiased information.
      • Additional Factors: Note any other reasons that may affect the reliability or relevance of the evidence.
    4. Scoring Evidence:
      • Assign a score to each piece of evidence based on its vetting (scale of 0.01 to 0.99). The score reflects the evidence’s overall credibility and impact on supporting or refuting the hypotheses.
    5. Key Assumption Check:
      • After collecting evidence, revisit the key assumptions underlying each hypothesis. Use the gathered evidence to validate or challenge these assumptions.
      • This step ensures that your analysis does not rest on unexamined or faulty premises, thereby enhancing the analytical rigor.
