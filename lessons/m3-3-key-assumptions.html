    Key Assumptions

    Key Assumptions Check

    A key assumption check is a critical part of the analytical process, especially when analyzing conspiracy theories, as it helps ensure that judgments and conclusions are based on valid premises. The primary goal is to identify and evaluate the assumptions that form the foundation of an analysis, often those accepted without scrutiny.

    The importance of a key assumption check lies in its ability to:

    1. Uncover hidden biases and faulty logic: By challenging assumptions, it prevents analysts from jumping to conclusions or making decisions based on incomplete or unexamined premises​

    2. Promote openness to new information: It encourages analysts to reconsider existing assumptions when new evidence arises, ensuring that conclusions remain flexible and adaptable​

    3. Enhance critical thinking: It forces the analyst to critically assess the validity of each assumption and its relevance, especially in light of evolving situations​

    4. Prevent overlooking important factors: Through systematic questioning, it can expose overlooked relationships and variables that may be key to understanding a situation​
       

    9 Questions down the rabbit hole with Key Assumptions

    Starting with Fiction

    1. What are some examples of key assumptions that characters in a movie or book believed to be true? How did these assumptions impact their decisions and the story’s outcome?
    2. Can you think of a time in a story where a character had to change their views because their assumptions were proven wrong? What led to that change?

    Bridging to Personal and Real-World Contexts

    1. What are some common sayings or beliefs that people often accept without question in our community or society? Why do you think they are rarely challenged?
    2. How does someone’s background, such as where they grow up or their family’s beliefs, influence the assumptions they make about others. Can you give an example?

    Delving into Controversial Topics

    1. Why might it be difficult to question or challenge assumptions that are tied to our identity, like your religion or the political beliefs of your family? How we answer questions like “why are poor people poor," and “why is terrorism more prevalent in Arab nations” go a long way to understanding key assumptions
    2. What are some potential consequences of never questioning assumptions about things like religion, politics, or social class?
    3. In what ways can questioning our assumptions lead to better understanding and relationships between people of different backgrounds? Can it sometimes cause conflict?

    Encouraging Personal Reflection and Growth

    1. Think about a belief or assumption you have had about someone or something that changed when you received new information. What was the belief, and what changed your mind?
    2. If you were to question one assumption that you have about your own community, religion, or family's political beliefs, what would it be and why? How would you go about finding more information?

    Example 1

    Hypothesis: The moon landing was faked.

    Key Assumptions and Their Evaluation:

    1. Assumption: The United States government had the resources and motive to fake the moon landing.
      • Why is this assumption correct? The theory suggests the U.S. government wanted to assert dominance in the space race during the Cold War and deceive the world into believing it had landed on the moon.
      • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Moderate to low confidence. While the U.S. had significant resources, faking such an event would require a vast conspiracy involving thousands of people, which seems unlikely to remain a secret for over 50 years.
      • What could invalidate this assumption? Revealing evidence, such as documentation or leaks, that the U.S. government was unable to keep such a secret would weaken this assumption.
      • Could it have been true in the past but false now? Possible. There might have been greater political pressure during the Cold War to appear technologically superior, but this would be less relevant today.
      • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If this assumption were invalid, it would significantly weaken the case for the moon landing being faked, as it suggests there was no plausible motivation for such a large-scale deception.
    2. Assumption: The technology of the 1960s was insufficient to land humans on the moon.
      • Why is this assumption correct? Some argue that the technology of the time (e.g., computers, rockets) was too primitive to achieve a moon landing.
      • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Low confidence. While technology was certainly more limited than today, many experts believe that the technology used was sufficient for a moon landing, and historical records support this.
      • What could invalidate this assumption? If new evidence showed that the rockets or spacecraft involved were fundamentally flawed or incapable of such a mission, it would weaken this assumption.
      • Could it have been true in the past but false now? Not really; historical and scientific evidence supports the feasibility of the mission.
      • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? Invalidating this assumption would undermine the core argument for the moon landing being faked, as it suggests the technological barrier wasn’t as insurmountable as claimed.
    3. Assumption: No independent or foreign organizations (e.g., Soviet Union) would have had the ability or desire to expose the hoax.
      • Why is this assumption correct? The theory suggests that during the Cold War, the U.S. could suppress or control any attempts by foreign nations to expose the fake landing.
      • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Moderate confidence. The Soviet Union would likely have been motivated to expose such a hoax, but there is no solid evidence they ever did.
      • What could invalidate this assumption? If evidence surfaced showing that foreign governments had been unable or unwilling to verify the moon landings or if they were actively involved in a cover-up, it would weaken this assumption.
      • Could it have been true in the past but false now? It could have been valid in the context of the Cold War, but with the passage of time, new transparency and international collaboration would have made such a conspiracy less likely.
      • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If this assumption were invalid, it would significantly undermine the claim of a successful cover-up, as international scrutiny would have exposed the deception.

    Conclusion:

    The key assumptions check helps reveal the logical structure of the conspiracy theory and challenges the validity of its premises. In this case, invalidating key assumptions—such as the motivation and feasibility of the moon landing hoax—would weaken the entire argument for the theory that the moon landing was faked. Conversely, if these assumptions were validated, it could make the hypothesis more plausible, but it still faces significant challenges in the form of contradictory evidence and scientific consensus.

    Example 2

    Hypothesis: The Pittsburgh Steelers cheat at football and have always cheated at football.

    1. Assumption: The Pittsburgh Steelers have a long history of cheating in their games.

    • Why is this assumption correct? The assumption is based on anecdotal evidence, media reports, and stories of questionable calls during games.
    • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Moderate confidence. While some instances may be interpreted as cheating, many accusations are unproven or based on biases.
    • What could invalidate this assumption? If thorough investigations and evidence showed no consistent pattern of cheating across their history, this assumption would be invalid.
    • Could this assumption have been true in the past but false now? Possible. Some past actions might have been seen as controversial, but it’s important to differentiate between cheating and controversial tactics.
    • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If this assumption were found invalid, it would cast doubt on the entire claim of the Steelers being habitual cheaters.
    • Score: 0.4 (Moderately supported, but there is no consistent evidence of historical cheating)

    2. Assumption: The Steelers are given favorable treatment by referees because of their reputation and fanbase.

    • Why is this assumption correct? Some argue that referees, influenced by crowd pressure or media narratives, may show bias toward popular teams with large fanbases like the Steelers.
    • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Low confidence. While there might be occasional bias in favor of teams with large followings, there's no concrete evidence that the Steelers are given systematic favorable treatment.
    • What could invalidate this assumption? Referee reports, review of game footage, or analysis showing no bias toward the Steelers would weaken this assumption.
    • Could this assumption have been true in the past but false now? It could have been more plausible in earlier decades, but more recent efforts to reduce referee bias make this assumption weaker today.
    • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If proven false, it would weaken the idea that the Steelers benefit from referee bias and lessen the overall argument.
    • Score: 0.3 (This assumption is based on perception, not solid evidence)

    3. Assumption: Other NFL teams and their players have publicly stated that the Steelers cheat.

    • Why is this assumption correct? This assumption could be based on interviews or statements from rival players or coaches suggesting the Steelers have gained unfair advantages.
    • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Low confidence. There are occasional criticisms or claims of unfair tactics, but these are generally not verified as cheating.
    • What could invalidate this assumption? If no reputable player or team member can provide concrete evidence or accusations of cheating, this assumption becomes invalid.
    • Could this assumption have been true in the past but false now? Past rivalries might have generated rumors, but any serious accusations would need strong evidence.
    • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If no credible accusations exist, this assumption falls apart and weakens the claim that the Steelers have always cheated.
    • Score: 0.2 (Very weak, based on limited or unverified sources)

    4. Assumption: The Steelers have used performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs) to gain an unfair advantage.

    • Why is this assumption correct? Some may point to individual players who were caught for PEDs as evidence of systemic cheating in the organization.
    • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Low confidence. While individual cases exist, there is no evidence that PED use is a long-standing, team-wide practice.
    • What could invalidate this assumption? If players are found to have used PEDs independently of team knowledge or direction, the assumption would not hold.
    • Could this assumption have been true in the past but false now? Possible, as PED use has been more thoroughly monitored in recent years.
    • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? If this assumption is disproven, it undermines the argument that the Steelers are systematic cheaters.
    • Score: 0.3 (Weak, as it focuses on isolated incidents)

    5. Assumption: The Steelers’ Super Bowl victories were influenced by illegal tactics or cheating.

    • Why is this assumption correct? Some argue that the team’s success is the result of rule-bending, such as illegal hits, strategic fouls, or game manipulation.
    • How confident am I that this assumption is valid? Very low confidence. There is no verified evidence to suggest that any Super Bowl victories were marred by cheating.
    • What could invalidate this assumption? Detailed reviews of game footage and fair investigations showing that no illegal tactics were used would invalidate this assumption.
    • Could this assumption have been true in the past but false now? Unlikely. Super Bowl victories are rigorously analyzed and reviewed, and no substantial evidence of cheating has been found.
    • How would this assumption’s invalidation affect the analysis? Disproving this assumption would significantly weaken the overall hypothesis that the Steelers are cheaters.
    • Score: 0.1 (Very weak, as there is no credible evidence supporting this claim)

    Summary of Scores:

    • Assumption 1: 0.4
    • Assumption 2: 0.3
    • Assumption 3: 0.2
    • Assumption 4: 0.3
    • Assumption 5: 0.1

    Total Score: 1.3/5 (Weak overall support for the hypothesis, with multiple assumptions being poorly supported or based on subjective claims)

    The Conspiracy Theory Industrial Complex: Monetization and Markets.

    The Emergence of Conspiracy Theories as Business

    • Historical Context: Initially, conspiracy theories were limited to niche groups and not widely accepted or known.
    • Role of the Internet: The digital age has propelled these theories into the mainstream, providing a platform for rapid dissemination and community building.
    • Case Study: Alex Jones: Founder of InfoWars, Jones transformed his media platform into a hub for conspiracy theories, significantly influencing their commercialization.

    Revenue Streams from Conspiracy Theories

    • Direct Sales: InfoWars, for example, sells a range of products from survival gear to health supplements, directly linking merchandise to the theories broadcasted.
      • Example: During the COVID-19 pandemic, Jones advertised products like dietary supplements, which he misleadingly claimed could ward off the virus.
    • Advertising Revenue: Websites hosting conspiracy content often see high traffic volumes, which translate to substantial ad revenue.
    • Subscription Models: Some creators offer additional content for a fee, including exclusive videos and articles, fostering a gated community feeling.

    Economic Markets and Consumer Behavior

    • Target Market Analysis: Typically, consumers of conspiracy theory content are individuals who distrust mainstream media and are looking for alternative explanations.
    • Psychological Drivers: Factors such as fear and the allure of being privy to 'forbidden knowledge' motivate consumption.
      • Example: Merchandise related to QAnon exploits the desire for belonging to a cause, evidenced by apparel and accessories emblazoned with cryptic symbols.

    Impact of Conspiracy Theories on Traditional Markets

    • Mainstream Business Impact: Some businesses find themselves inadvertently caught in conspiracy theories, which can lead to boycotts or unwanted attention.
      • Example: The Pizzagate conspiracy led to a significant, direct impact on Comet Ping Pong, a pizzeria in Washington, D.C., including a violent incident involving a gunman.
    • Stock Market and Commodity Prices: At times, false information can lead to temporary stock volatility, affecting investments and perceptions.

    Legal and Ethical Considerations

    • Legal Challenges: Figures like Alex Jones have faced numerous legal challenges due to the content they promote, balancing on the edge of free speech and harmful misinformation.
    • Ethical Dilemmas: Platforms that host and profit from conspiracy content wrestle with the ethical implications of their revenue strategies, especially when such content causes real-world harm.

    Looking Forward

    • Future Trends: We are likely to see an evolution in how conspiracy theories are monetized, with new technologies and platforms playing pivotal roles.
    • Navigational Strategies: Both consumers and businesses must develop strategies to deal with misinformation—emphasizing critical thinking and verification.
